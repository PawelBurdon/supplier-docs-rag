QUALITY INSPECTION PROCEDURE
Document reference: HCS-QIP-2023
Owner: Halden Cycle Supply B.V., Warehouse Quality
Version 1.4, revised 22 August 2023

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This procedure describes incoming goods inspection at the Rotterdam warehouse.
It applies to every inbound lot from every supplier, including consignment
stock and free-of-charge warranty replacements. It also applies to goods
returned under the Returns and Warranty Policy (HCS-RWP-2025), where it is used
to decide whether a claim is valid.

2. DEFINITIONS

Lot: the quantity of a single SKU received against a single delivery note.
Critical defect: a defect that could reasonably cause injury in normal use, for
  example a weld crack, a delaminated carbon layup, or a missing safety washer.
Major defect: a defect that renders the unit unsaleable but is not a safety
  risk, for example a bent derailleur hanger or a mismatched paint batch.
Minor defect: a cosmetic issue that does not prevent sale at full price.

3. SAMPLING PLAN

Sampling follows ISO 2859-1, general inspection level II, single sampling,
normal severity. Acceptance quality limits are:

  Critical defects: AQL 0.0 - a single critical defect rejects the lot.
  Major defects:    AQL 1.0
  Minor defects:    AQL 2.5

Lot size to sample size, level II:
  51 to 90 units        sample 13
  91 to 150 units       sample 20
  151 to 280 units      sample 32
  281 to 500 units      sample 50
  501 to 1200 units     sample 80

Severity is tightened to ISO 2859-1 tightened inspection after two rejected
lots from the same supplier within any rolling six-month period, and returns to
normal after five consecutive accepted lots.

4. INSPECTION STEPS

4.1 Documentation check. Confirm the delivery note references a valid purchase
order number and that the SKU codes match. A delivery note without a purchase
order number is quarantined, not rejected, and the category buyer is notified
the same day.

4.2 Packaging check. Confirm outer cartons are undamaged and that units are
individually protected. Transit damage is photographed before unpacking.

4.3 Identification check. Confirm each sampled unit carries a legible serial
number and that the lot number on the carton matches the delivery note. Lot
numbers are recorded in the warehouse system and are the link used for supplier
recovery of warranty costs.

4.4 Dimensional check. Measure head tube angle, seat tube length and bottom
bracket shell width on each sampled frame against the drawing in Schedule A of
the relevant framework agreement. Tolerance is plus or minus 0.5 degrees on
angles and plus or minus 0.3 millimetres on lengths.

4.5 Thread and interface check. Verify bottom bracket and derailleur hanger
threads with a go/no-go gauge. Verify that the fork steerer diameter matches
the specification for the SKU.

4.6 Torque verification. On two units per lot, tighten the derailleur hanger
bolt to the printed specification with a calibrated torque wrench and confirm
no thread deformation. Torque wrenches are calibrated every 6 months and the
calibration certificate is kept for 3 years.

4.7 Carbon lot check. For FRM-CRB and FRK-CRB lots, tap-test the head tube and
bottom bracket junctions of every sampled unit. Any dull response escalates to
ultrasonic inspection by the external laboratory before the lot is released.

5. DISPOSITION

Accepted lots are booked into sellable stock the same working day.
Rejected lots are moved to the quarantine area and are not booked into stock.
Quality Assurance raises a non-conformance report within 2 business days,
containing photographs, the sampling record, and the defect classification.

The supplier must respond with a containment action within 5 business days and
a root cause analysis within 15 business days. Failure to respond within these
windows is treated as acceptance of the rejection.

Rejected lots are returned at the supplier's cost, or scrapped at the
supplier's cost where return freight would exceed the lot value. The
Distributor does not rework supplier goods without written agreement and an
agreed rework rate.

6. RECORDS

Inspection records are retained for 7 years. Records include the sampling
record, defect classification, photographs, the inspector's identity, and the
disposition decision. Records are auditable by the supplier on 10 business
days' notice.
