PACKAGING AND LABELLING SPECIFICATION
Document reference: HCS-PLS-2025
Owner: Halden Cycle Supply B.V., Warehouse Operations
Version 1.1, issued 6 May 2025
Applies to: all inbound deliveries from all suppliers

1. PURPOSE

This specification defines how goods must be packed, labelled and palletised for
delivery to the Rotterdam warehouse. It exists because packaging defects are the
most common cause of a delivery being quarantined at receipt, and because the
inspection procedure HCS-QIP-2023 checks packaging condition but does not say
what correct packaging is.

2. OUTER CARTONS

2.1 Cartons are double-wall corrugated board, minimum 5 mm, with a bursting
strength of at least 1.5 MPa.

2.2 No carton may exceed 23 kilograms gross. A heavier consignment is split
across cartons rather than reinforced.

2.3 Cartons are sealed with paper tape. Plastic tape is not accepted, because
the warehouse separates card and plastic at goods-in and paper tape allows the
carton to go straight to the card stream.

3. UNIT PROTECTION

3.1 Frame sets travel in a moulded pulp cradle at the head tube and bottom
bracket, with a foam sleeve on each tube.

3.2 Carbon frame sets and carbon forks additionally travel in a sealed moisture
barrier bag with one desiccant sachet per unit. A carbon unit arriving without
its barrier bag is quarantined, not rejected, and released only after the
carbon lot check in HCS-QIP-2023 section 4.7.

3.3 Wheelsets travel with an axle spacer fitted and the rotor mount masked.

3.4 Cassettes and chains travel in individual boxes inside the outer carton. A
loose cassette in an outer carton is a packaging defect, classified as minor
under HCS-QIP-2023 unless the unit itself is damaged.

4. LABELLING

4.1 Each outer carton carries a label showing: the purchase order number, the
SKU code, the quantity, the lot number, and the supplier name.

4.2 The lot number on the carton label must match the lot number on the
delivery note. This is the link the warehouse uses to attribute a later warranty
claim to a supplier, and a mismatch is treated as a documentation failure at
incoming inspection.

4.3 Labels are printed at 300 dpi or better, on white matt stock, in the lower
right quadrant of the largest face. Handwritten labels are not accepted.

4.4 Barcodes are Code 128, minimum 30 millimetres tall, with a quiet zone of at
least 6 millimetres. A barcode that fails to scan twice at goods-in is
relabelled by the warehouse at the supplier's cost.

5. PALLETS

5.1 Pallets are EUR-pallets, 1200 by 800 millimetres, in sound condition.

5.2 Maximum loaded pallet height is 1800 millimetres including the pallet
itself. Maximum loaded pallet weight is 700 kilograms.

5.3 Cartons are stacked square to the pallet edges with no overhang. Overhanging
cartons are the second most common quarantine reason after unreadable labels.

5.4 Loads are secured with stretch wrap and two horizontal straps. Metal banding
is not accepted, because it damages the carton edge and the warehouse's
de-palletising equipment.

6. CONSEQUENCES OF NON-CONFORMANCE

6.1 A packaging or labelling defect does not by itself reject a lot. The lot is
quarantined, the category buyer is notified the same day, and the supplier is
given the option to have the goods relabelled or repacked at its own cost.

6.2 Repeated packaging non-conformance — four quarantined deliveries within any
rolling twelve-month period — is grounds for a written performance notice under
the Delivery SLA.

6.3 Relabelling by the warehouse is charged at EUR 45 per pallet. Repacking is
charged at cost and is only offered where the goods themselves are undamaged.

7. RECORDS

Photographs of any packaging defect are retained with the incoming inspection
record for the same 7 years as the inspection record itself.
